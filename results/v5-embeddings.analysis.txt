2. TRUNCATION — the confound inside any v4-vs-v5 comparison
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  window            256 wordpieces (the checkpoint's own limit, not a choice)
  truncated         2566 of 27325 documents (9.4%)
  longest document  6781 wordpieces
  mean after trunc  123.5
  text template     title + "\n" + body

  BM25 reads every token of a 2,185-token document; this model reads the
  first 256. That asymmetry sits inside the rung comparison and cannot be
  removed, only measured — which is what the 128-wordpiece run is for.

3. THE VECTORS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  norms         min 1.5166 · p50 2.8798 · p95 4.3206 · max 7.0002
  stored        unnormalised, so `normalise` is a param flip rather than a re-embed
  mean pairwise cosine (sampled at embed time)  0.1272 over 128271 pairs
  max  pairwise cosine (sampled at embed time)  0.7965

  A mean pairwise cosine near 1 would mean the embedding had collapsed and
  any nDCG from it was an artifact. embed-corpus.js hard-fails above 0.9.
  The spread of norms is 4.6x, which is why cosine-vs-dot is a real
  ablation here and not a formality.

4. INDEX BUILD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  five builds   49 36 36 39 36 ms
  median        36 ms      (v4-bm25: 982 ms · v3-tfidf: ~1,470 ms)

  v5 builds no postings, no vocabulary and no df table. It copies 42 MB into

  work the other rungs do at index time was done once by the embed step.

5. MEMORY — three numbers that must not be conflated
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  typed arrays, computed from the structures    40.24 MiB
    matrix  27325 x 384 x 4B = 40.03 MiB · norms 27325 x 8B = 0.21 MiB
  retained for one handle (--expose-gc)         42.2 MiB
    of which V8 heap      2.0 MiB   the id Map and the idByIndex array
    of which arrayBuffers 40.2 MiB   the matrix and the norms
    (handle held live across the measurement: 27325 docs)

    BOTH TERMS ARE REPORTED BECAUSE heapUsed ALONE IS MISLEADING HERE, and
    it was the first thing this script got wrong. A large typed array's
    backing store is allocated OUTSIDE the V8 heap, so heapUsed reports
    ~2 MiB for a structure that is 40 MiB of floats. v4's 34.8 MiB
    retained-heap figure (§16.12) did not have this problem — its Maps and
    strings are genuinely on the heap — so the two are NOT comparable
    quantities and reading them side by side would be a mistake.
  peak RSS for this process                     325 MiB

  The third is dominated by the parsed corpus, which EVERY rung pays and
  which is not part of the index — §16.12 made the same separation for v4,
  where quoting RSS as "BM25's memory" would have been wrong by 19x.

6. SEARCH, AND WHY EXACT RATHER THAN ANN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  per query     mean 19.53 ms · p50 19.35 · p95 20.43 ms
  work          2 x 27325 x 384 = 21.0 MFLOP per query, plus a 27324-element sort
  effective     1.08 GFLOP/s at p50 — plain JS over a Float32Array, no SIMD intrinsics

  Exact search visits every document. An ANN index would buy sublinear
  search and cost three things:
    1. a dependency, inside the step this phase is about pinning;
    2. build-time hyperparameters (HNSW M, efConstruction) needing their own
       sweep and their own selection-bias accounting — §13.7 put 44% of v4's
       tuning margin down to optimism, so that is not a formality;
    3. APPROXIMATE RECALL, a new error source placed INSIDE the thing being
       measured. A v4-vs-v5 difference would then confound the retriever with
       the index, and CLAUDE.md's one-variable rule would be broken by the
       data structure rather than by the experiment.
  ANN starts paying when N x dim stops fitting the latency budget, around
  N > 1e6. This corpus is 27325, and a full scan costs 19 ms.

7. HOW DIFFERENT ARE v4 AND v5? — the number 3.5 actually needs
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  mean Jaccard of the two top-8 sets    0.1906
  queries where the top-8 sets are DISJOINT   221 of 2304 (9.6%)

  RRF pays when two systems fail on different queries. This is the
  measurement of "different" — recorded at 3.4 so 3.5 designs against a
  number rather than discovering it in its own results.
