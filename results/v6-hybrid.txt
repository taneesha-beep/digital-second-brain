2. THE ARITHMETIC, AUDITED BECAUSE THE RUNG LOSES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

§17.6 audited a surprisingly good number on the principle that it is the one
nobody feels like checking. A surprisingly bad one is the same risk mirrored, so
the fusion was cross-checked at corpus scale against artifacts written by a
different code path in earlier sessions:

  v6 at depth 10  vs  a hand fusion of v4's and v5's top-10 committed run files
    agree     2304 of 2304 queries
    disagree  0

At depth 10 the component lists ARE those two files, so this reproduces v6 out
of results/runs/v4-bm25.dev.run and results/runs/v5-embeddings.dev.run. It is
also what makes "just read two run files" unnecessary rather than merely
rejected: in-index fusion computes what run-file fusion would, so refusing the
cheap implementation gave nothing up.

At fixture scale, tests/retrieval.v6-hybrid.test.js ranks each component INSIDE
v6 and against a standalone handle over all 34 documents at full depth and
requires identical orderings; checks fused scores against hand-computed
reciprocals; and covers the depth and minmax-sum semantics. 227 tests across 12
suites, up from 196 across 11.

Four eval runs emitted BYTE-IDENTICAL output (cmp-verified against
f730d1b1…), so the metrics cannot move and only the wall time does.


3. THE REGISTERED COMPARISON — and the shape is the finding
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
results/comparisons/v6-hybrid-vs-v5-embeddings.dev.txt, id "ladder-v5-v6"

  nDCG@8    A 0.3114   B 0.3269   A-B  -0.015532  [-0.025316, -0.005800]  p 0.0015

  1044 of 2304 queries moved
    v6 ahead on   534   mean gain  +0.248333
    v5 ahead on   510   mean loss  -0.330188

V6 WINS ON MORE QUERIES THAN V5 AND STILL LOSES, because v5's wins are 33%
larger. That is the convexity argument from the prediction, measured: with a
median of one judgment per query, nDCG@8 is largely "where did the single right
answer land", and 1/log2(r+1) is convex.

Concretely at rrfK = 60, a document v4 ranks 1st and v5 ranks 20th scores
1/61 + 1/80 = 0.0289, beating one v5 ranks 1st and v4 never reaches at
1/61 = 0.0164. The better system loses that exchange by construction.

ALSO REPORTED, p SUPPRESSED because only adjacent rungs are registered (§11.5):

  v6-hybrid vs v4-bm25   +0.065601  [+0.057013, +0.074101]  723 up / 215 down

So v6 sits between its two components. Roadmap 3.5 asks for "significance vs
both v4 and v5"; this is what that can honestly mean — full difference and
interval, no p-value, because that comparison was not the experiment.


4. THE PREMISE INVERTED — where the loss actually comes from
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.4 handed 3.5 a number and an instruction: v4 and v5 agree on nothing on 221
of 2304 queries, and "RRF pays exactly when systems fail on different queries,
so 3.5 designs against a number". Split dev on exactly that partition:

  stratum          n      v5 nDCG@8    v6 nDCG@8     v6 - v5
  disjoint       221       0.210643     0.152709    -0.057934
  overlapping   2083       0.339227     0.328194    -0.011034

ON THE 221 QUERIES RRF IS SUPPOSED TO BE FOR, FUSION DOES FIVE TIMES THE DAMAGE
IT DOES EVERYWHERE ELSE. The premise is not merely unmet, it is backwards.

Complementarity is NECESSARY for fusion to pay and nowhere near SUFFICIENT. The
other condition is that neither component dominates, and v5 beats v4 by 0.0811.
When two systems of unequal strength disagree completely, the better one is
usually the one that is right, and unweighted RRF hands the worse one half the
slots.

Where the fused top-8 comes from, 18,432 documents over 2304 queries:

  in BOTH components' top-8      5500   29.8%
  in the DENSE top-8 only        4546   24.7%
  in the BM25 top-8 only         4696   25.5%
  promoted from below BOTH       3690   20.0%

A quarter of every fused list is BM25's alone, on a rung where BM25 scores
0.0811 below the alternative. The 20% promoted from below both cut-offs is
fusion doing the thing fusion is for, and it does not pay for the rest.

AND THE LENGTH DISTRIBUTION CLOSES A LOOP §17.9 OPENED. Term length of retrieved
documents, top 8 over the whole dev run:

  d          <=9    10-19    20-49      50+
  v5         675     3943     9019     4795
  v6         488     3435     9378     5131

§17.9 measured v5 retrieving 1.50x as many very short documents as v4 and named
that as the mechanism of v5's win: a short question whose few words miss the
query is INVISIBLE to BM25 — no postings reach it — while its embedding sits
wherever its meaning sits. v6 retrieves 0.72x as many as v5. It gives back
roughly a third of exactly the advantage that made v5 the best rung.


5. THE ABLATIONS — each ONE variable, both EXPLORATORY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(a) THE FUSION FUNCTION — fusion, and the question §16.11 asked a rung ahead.

  nDCG@8   0.311965 vs 0.311361   +0.000604   CI [-0.004541, +0.005812]
  667 of 2304 moved; minmax-sum ahead on 298, rrf on 369

A NULL, AND THE LOAD-BEARING ABLATION OF THE RUNG. 3.3's noticed-list warned
that RRF discards score magnitude — the class qtfMode priced at +0.045597,
larger than the entire v3->v4 rung — so a score-preserving fusion was the
obvious rescue. CombSUM over per-query min-max normalised component scores
rescues nothing: the interval straddles zero, and v6 loses to v5 by ~0.0155
under BOTH fusion functions.

So THE LOSS IS NOT CAUSED BY FUSING ON RANK RATHER THAN ON SCORE. The warning
was reasonable and about the wrong thing. Why it was wrong is worth more than
the warning: qtfMode's magnitude lives INSIDE one scorer's arithmetic, where it
decides which document wins; a fusion's magnitude only decides how two
already-decided orderings interleave.

Under minmax-sum the rrfK param is inert. It stays in the digest anyway — a
param that silently vanishes from a configuration is §13.10's "run that lies
about itself" in another costume.

(b) FUSION DEPTH — depth, the one arbitrary choice in the design.

  nDCG@8   0.310715 vs 0.311361   -0.000646   CI [-0.001381, -0.000048]
  6 of 2304 moved; full ahead on 5, depth-100 on 1

SIX QUERIES. The tail of each component list is worth essentially nothing at
k=10, which the arithmetic predicts — a document at rank 200 in both lists
scores 2/260, under a single rank-8 contribution of 1/68 — but it was a param
and an assumption, so it was measured rather than argued. Measurably nonzero,
practically nil. It also buys back 27% of the latency, which is the only place
in this rung where a knob does something useful.

NOT RUN, AND NAMED RATHER THAN LEFT SILENT: per-component weights. Unweighted
RRF is what 3.5 and the literature name, and given §4 a weight favouring the
dense half is the obvious next move — but it is a second continuous knob with
its own selection-bias bill. On the noticed-list for 3.6.

WHY THERE IS NO CHAIN, AND IT IS A DECISION MADE BEFORE THE RUN. §16.7 built a
telescoping chain for v3->v4; §17.5 declined one for v4->v5 because the
intermediate retrievers HAVE NO DEFINITION. v6 is a third case: the
intermediates exist and ARE ALREADY RUNGS.

  RRF over one list is that list. 1/(rrfK + rank) is strictly decreasing in
  rank and ranks within one list are distinct, so the induced order IS the
  rank order. RRF(dense alone) === v5. RRF(bm25 alone) === v4.

A chain would be one step that is identically zero by construction followed by
one step that is the whole registered margin. Unlike §17.5's decision, this
premise is testable and was tested: single-component RRF reproduces v4's and
v5's full-depth fixture orderings at rrfK in {0, 1, 60, 1000}.

7. SYMMETRY — RANK IS A PROPERTY OF A LIST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  v1 false · v2 true · v3 true · v4 false · v5 true · v6 FALSE

Declared in the module before the test was written, and measured by
tests/retrieval.symmetry.test.js in both directions.

§17.10 predicted this and attributed it to BM25's half. True, and it understates
the case in a way that matters, because the understatement suggests a fix that
does not exist:

  score_v6(A->B) reads B's position in the two lists ranked against A.
  score_v6(B->A) reads A's position in two DIFFERENT lists.

So a rank fusion of two PERFECTLY SCORE-SYMMETRIC retrievers is still
asymmetric. Measured on v5 alone, in the same file that proves v5's scores
symmetric at exact float equality: its RANKS disagree in both directions across
the fixture. B is A's nearest neighbour while A is not B's.

Symmetrising RRF would mean scoring f(A,B) + f(B,A), ranking the corpus from
both endpoints at n× the per-query cost. A different retriever, not a setting.

THE DECLARATION EARNED ITS KEEP THE FIRST TIME IT WAS INHERITED. It was added at
3.4 so the next rung would read a field instead of remembering a paragraph. 3.5
read v4 false · v5 true, worked out that neither value predicts the fused one,
and wrote false before running anything.

THE MIDDLE ROW WAS WRONG THE FIRST TIME AND THE CORRECTION IS RECORDED RATHER
THAN QUIETLY APPLIED — the third time this repo has had to separate heap from
arrayBuffers. Measured with the five timing handles still pending collection, it
reported 1.4 MiB of arrayBuffers for a structure containing a 40 MiB matrix: V8
releases external backing stores a collection LATER than the objects pointing at
them, so the "before" snapshot was inflated by garbage the "after" snapshot had
already released and the delta came out near zero. Dropping the timing handle
and collecting twice gives 55.5 MiB, which agrees with the 56.56 MiB computed
independently from the structures. The two numbers now check each other, which
is why both are printed.

v6 is the rung where the trap is worst because it MIXES the two: v4's retained
figure (§16.12, 34.8 MiB) is genuinely heap and v5's (§17.11, 40.2 MiB) is
genuinely not, so either term alone would be wrong in a different direction.

The RSS row comes from the RUN's sidecar, not from the analysis process, which
builds six indexes to time them and peaks at 740 MiB — a different quantity, not
comparable to §16.12's 650 or §17.11's 325.


9. WHAT THE RUNG EXPOSED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- REGISTRY.JSON'S FIRST UNFAVOURABLE PAYOFF. Pre-registration was written at 2.5
  to stop a favourable result being manufactured, and until now every registered
  comparison it constrained went the way the project hoped. This one did not,
  and the machinery produced the uncomfortable number with no discretion
  available at the point it mattered. That is the only kind of evidence that a
  pre-registration is real.

- A FORWARD WARNING CAN BE RIGHT ABOUT THE MECHANISM AND WRONG ABOUT THE EFFECT.
  §16.11 flagged RRF's magnitude loss a rung in advance; §5a measured it at a
  null. The value was in making the question cheap to answer.

- COMPLEMENTARITY IS NECESSARY, NOT SUFFICIENT, and 3.4 handed 3.5 the number
  with the wrong inference attached. §4. The missing condition — neither
  component dominating — is not exotic and is not in the sentence everyone
  repeats about hybrid retrieval.

- THE INTERFACE NEEDED ONE THING AND IT WAS A NAME, NOT A FEATURE. v6 is the
  first rung whose INPUT is a rank, so it is the first that has to know the
  tie-break. compareHits moved from a private expression in index.js to
  types.js; index.js still owns the ordering, and there is still one definition.
  Nothing else moved — no new signature, no runner change. run-eval.js was not
  touched at all, which is the §7.1 boundary and the flat-params decision paying
  off together.

- A THIRD PLACE SELF-RETRIEVAL HAS TO BE EXCLUDED, this time INSIDE one rank().
  v6 cannot hand the real ctx to both components, so each gets a private
  collector that sits outside index.js's mechanism 1 and has to replicate its
  contract. Tested at fixture scale, cross-checked at corpus scale.

- THE RUNNER, THE METRICS AND THE BOOTSTRAP: NOTHING. No new flag, no new
  convention. Every named run re-scored from its written run file and asserted
  against its committed sidecar at exact float equality.

- ONE PROCESS FAILURE, THE SAME ONE 3.4 RECORDED, CAUGHT BY THE SAME HABIT. The
  two ablation ABSOLUTES in §5 were first written as 0.311971 and 0.310689 —
  derived by adding the report's delta to a four-decimal display rather than
  read from the sidecar. The true values are 0.311965 and 0.310715. The deltas
  and intervals were always correct because they came from the reports; only
  the levels were extrapolated. Nothing downstream rests on them and no
  conclusion moves, which is exactly why it is the kind of error that survives:
  §17's note that 3.4 quoted "digits past the fourth extrapolated from a rounded
  display" describes this precisely, one rung later. Caught by re-deriving every
  quoted figure from the JSON before the writeup was pushed. The check is worth
  more than the fix — it should be a script, and it is not one yet.

- THE SWEEP CSV NO LONGER CARRIES p95SearchMs — 3.3's noticed-list item, closed
  for new tools. sweep-v4.js deliberately still carries it, since removing it
  there desynchronises a banked CSV unless a 1,327 s grid is re-run for a
  cosmetic change. The defect stops propagating; removing it from the committed
  artifact is 3.6's.


10. WHAT THIS RUNG DOES NOT ESTABLISH
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- NOT A TEST-SET RESULT. Dev only, until 3.6.

- NOT HOLM-ADJUSTED. The p is raw; six of the family's seven now exist. 0.0015
  clears alpha/7 = 0.00714 by a factor of 4.8 — far less headroom than the three
  rungs sitting at the bootstrap floor, so this is the FIRST rung whose Holm
  outcome is worth re-checking when the family closes at 3.6.

- NOT "HYBRID RETRIEVAL DOES NOT WORK". It is one fusion (RRF, plus one CombSUM
  ablation), over one pair of components whose strengths differ by 0.0811, on
  one positive-only key with a median of one judgment per query. §4 names the
  condition that failed; a pair of comparable strength is a different experiment
  and this says nothing about it.

- NOT A VERDICT ON WEIGHTED FUSION. Unweighted RRF is what was measured.

- NOT A CLAIM THAT CONVEXITY IS THE WHOLE STORY. §3's win/loss asymmetry is
  consistent with that argument and is not a proof of it; §4's length
  distribution is a second, independent mechanism.

- ABSOLUTES REMAIN LOWER BOUNDS (§5.1), and §17.6's amendment applies with more
  force here rather than less: v6's lists are half dense, so they inherit the
  dense rung's extra ways of being right that this key cannot see.
