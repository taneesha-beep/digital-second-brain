The first honest opponent. v1, v2 and v3 all sit downstream of one decade-old
product constant — the top-10 keyword list, or a threshold calibrated against
it — so every rung comparison so far has been partly a measurement of that
constant. BM25 is 1994, it is what every new retrieval system is still measured
against, and its two knobs were chosen on TREC collections that are not this
one.

  IT WINS. v4-bm25 beats v3-tfidf by 0.0351 nDCG@8, 95% CI [0.0255, 0.0449],
  p < 0.0001. The second rung to clear Holm's alpha/7 = 0.00714, and it clears
  it at the bootstrap floor.

SAID PLAINLY, BECAUSE IT IS THE UNCOMFORTABLE READING AND IT IS THE TRUE ONE:
a 1994 algorithm at its textbook defaults, with no tuning on this corpus at
all, beats the retriever this project spent three sessions building. v3 was not
a wasted rung — it is +0.068 over v2 and it is what made the ladder's
similarity-function finding legible — but as a retriever it is beaten by the
standard baseline, and the margin is not close to the resolution limit.

3. WHAT v4 IS, AND THE PARAM THE LADDER LOST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    score(q,D) = SUM over t in q&D of

                        idf(t) . qw(t) . ------ tf(t,D).(k1+1) ------
                                          tf(t,D) + k1.(1-b+b.|D|/avgdl)

  FIVE PARAMS.

    k1          1.2       tf saturation. Textbook default, deliberately NOT
                          selected on this corpus — section 4.
    b           0.75      length normalisation, pivoted between none and full.
    idfVariant  lucene    ln(1 + (N-df+0.5)/(df+0.5)) — section 6.
    qtfMode     linear    Robertson's k3 at its limits: linear is k3 -> inf
                          (qw = qtf), binary is k3 = 0 (qw = 1).
    titleWeight 2         the inherited title doubling, made explicit.

  WHAT IS INHERITED, AND WHY EACH IS DEFENSIBLE RATHER THAN COPIED.

  tokenise() + STOPWORDS   from v1, unchanged. Non-negotiable: see section 1.

  the df table             IDENTICAL to v3's, asserted by a test rather than
                           argued. Both count one document per term from
                           new Set(tokenise(title + ' ' + body)), so the title
                           doubling cannot reach a Set. Only the FORMULA
                           mapping df to idf changed, not the counts. That is
                           what keeps "v3 vs v4" a scoring comparison.

  titleWeight 2            §15.1's principle — a rung changes how a PAIR is
                           scored, not how a DOCUMENT is read. Kept, and priced
                           by an ablation (section 5), because it is newly
                           interesting: under BM25 the doubling feeds |D| as
                           well as tf, which it never did before. Measured, the
                           doubling is 9.2% of every length BM25 divides by
                           (avgdl 53.9953 at titleWeight 2, 49.0017 at 1).

  WHAT DID NOT CARRY, AND THE FIRST ONE IS THE METHODOLOGICAL CONTENT.

  idfCorpus  DROPPED. THIS IS THE ONE PARAM HELD CONSTANT v1 -> v2 -> v3, and
             3.3 is where the chain breaks. Not for convenience: it has NO
             REFERENT. §15.1's exactness argument works because a document's
             vector holds only terms it CONTAINS, so df_loo = df - 1 for every
             such document and one global table is exact. BM25's idf multiplies
             a (query, document) pair where BOTH contain t, so "leave one out"
             is ambiguous between df-1 (exclude the query) and df-2 (exclude
             both), with no principled tiebreak.

             The price is MEASURED rather than waved at, and it is not
             uniformly negligible:

               max |idf(df) - idf(df-1)| over df >= 2   0.510826  at df 2
               at df = 5150 ("recipe")                  0.00019417
               at df = 100                              0.01000008
               at df = 10                               0.10008346
               at df = 2                                0.51082562

             Negligible across the mass of the vocabulary, a whole half-nat at
             the rare-term end — which is where duplicate detection lives. So
             the drop is a decision with a cost, stated, not a simplification.

  lengthBonus  DROPPED. It only ordered terms for slice(0, topN) and there is no
               topN. §15.4b already proved it inert at full vocabulary by a
               byte-identical run.
  topN         DROPPED. Full vocabulary by construction; §15.4a priced the
               truncation at +0.0289 on v3.
  minShared    DROPPED. §15.4c priced it on v3 at +0.0023 with an interval
               straddling zero, and v3 declined it. Same structure here —
               candidates come from the query's postings — so carrying the
               handle would re-ask a settled question.
  threshold / scorePrecision / cap  ABSENT. §15.2's reasoning transfers
               unchanged, and `cap` was pre-registered absent FOR v4
               SPECIFICALLY at 3.1 (types.js, §14.3), before this file existed.

  AND A PROPERTY THE LADDER LOSES.

  v4 IS NOT SYMMETRIC. score(A->B) != score(B->A): the document side is
  length-normalised and saturated, the query side is neither. v2 ESTABLISHED
  symmetry as a property of the algorithm (§14.5, exact float equality, with v1
  put through the same test and failing it) and v3 preserved it at real cost
  (§15.8's sorted term ids). v4 gives it back.

  Measured rather than left as an omission, in the same shape §14.5 used:
  190 of 197 reachable fixture pairs disagree between directions. Every
  symmetric exception has |A| = |B|, which is necessary — with unequal lengths
  the two directions evaluate different saturation curves. Equal length is not
  SUFFICIENT: documents 025 and 033 share `brine` (3 vs 1) and `poultry`
  (1 vs 3) at equal length and are symmetric anyway, because those two terms
  carry the same df and the contributions swap without changing the sum. A
  structural coincidence, and worth knowing the formula permits it.

  PHASE 4 INHERITS THE TENSION DIRECTLY. Bidirectional note links are what v2
  was built for; the rung that beats it is asymmetric, so a v4 deployment
  re-introduces the direction-dependence v2 removed at the algorithm level.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Roadmap 3.3 says "start at k1 = 1.2, b = 0.75; tune on dev only". Two rulings
  already on the books decide what that can mean for the REGISTERED comparison:

    §14.2  "a swept v2 measured against an unswept v1 is not the registered
           experiment."
    §11.5  sweep-tuned-vs-shipped is registered on TEST, because "sweeps select;
           they do not test."

  v3 was never swept. So:

    ladder-v3-v4 IS DEFAULT BM25 (k1 1.2, b 0.75) AGAINST UNTUNED v3.

  Both sides carry zero cooking-dev selection and the p-value means what it
  says. The alternative would price "BM25 plus a 108-point search over the same

  THE DEFAULTS ARE NOT A HANDICAP. k1 = 1.2 / b = 0.75 carry a decade of
  selection — on other corpora. That is exactly what makes BM25 a fair opponent
  here rather than a weak one, and it is the difference between this rung and
  the three below it.

7. NEGATIVE IDF — WHAT WOULD BREAK, AND WHY assertHits WOULD NOT CATCH IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Robertson/Sparck-Jones is ln((N-df+0.5)/(df+0.5)), negative for df > N/2. It
  cannot fire on cooking, which is exactly why it would ship unnoticed. A
  note-taking app's own corpus is the kind that could easily have a term in most
  documents.

  MEASURED ON A CORPUS BUILT TO CROSS THE BOUNDARY (four documents, "salt" in
  three of them), in tests/retrieval.v4-bm25.test.js:

    - assertHits ACCEPTS A NEGATIVE-SCORING HIT. types.js checks
      Number.isFinite(score) and descending order. A negative score satisfies
      both. So a document that is EVIDENCE AGAINST the query is returned as a
      hit and written to the run file as a retrieval — an anti-match presented
      as a result, invisible to every postcondition the interface has. The test
      asserts the hits are still a well-formed, correctly ordered list, which
      is the point.

    - v3's [0,1] INVARIANT HAS NO ANALOGUE. v3 asserts 0 <= score <= 1+1e-9
      inside rank() because a cosine of non-negative vectors is bounded. BM25 is
      an unbounded sum with no bound at either end.

  WHAT v4 ASSERTS INSTEAD, which is what the formula actually guarantees:

    always            Number.isFinite(score), else throw
    under lucene      score > 0, else throw. Every idf is strictly positive and
                      every candidate shares at least one term, so a
                      non-positive score is a defect rather than an edge case.
    under robertson   finiteness only. A negative score there is the formula
                      working as designed, and asserting it away would hide the
                      behaviour the variant exists to expose.

  AND IT IS RECORDED RATHER THAN ASSUMED AWAY. buildIndex counts terms with
  idf <= 0 and the count reaches the handle; under lucene a non-zero count is
  impossible for df <= N and throws at index() naming the cause. On cooking the
  count is 0 under both variants — and that zero being in the provenance is the
  difference between "it does not happen here" and "nobody checked".

  Measured idf ranges, `npm run analyse:bm25`:

    lucene     [1.6687, 9.8101]   ratio 5.88x   non-positive terms 0
    robertson  [1.4599, 9.8101]   ratio 6.72x   non-positive terms 0
    v3-tfidf   [2.6688, 11.2156]  ratio 4.20x

  v3's idf enters the cosine SQUARED — once in the query weight, once in the
  document weight — so its effective rare-term emphasis is 17.66x against
  BM25's linear 5.88x. That is a large difference in how aggressively rare
  terms are favoured, and it is one of the behaviours section 8's first chain
  step bundles.


8. THE THREE-STEP CHAIN, WHICH CLOSES EXACTLY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The registered comparison moves several behaviours at once. 3.2 set the
  precedent (§15.5): a comparison that moves more than one behaviour gets a
  telescoping one-variable chain closing to the registered total at exact float
  equality. Six behaviours move here, and one of them is free:

    1. the idf formula                    ln(N/df)+1  ->  BM25's
    2. the idf EXPONENT                   squared in a cosine, linear in BM25
    3. the query-side weight              tf_q . idf  ->  qtf
    4. tf saturation                      linear  ->  k1
    5. document normalisation             L2 norm  ->  pivoted b
    6. query normalisation                ||q||  ->  none

  (6) IS FREE AND CAN BE SAID EXACTLY: ||q|| is constant within a query and
  every metric reads rank order within a query, so dropping it is
  rank-preserving. It changes the score column's scale and nothing that is
  measured. Six become five.

    step                                       nDCG@8        delta
    v3-tfidf                                  0.210660
    -> v4-bm25-flat   (k1 -> linear, b = 0)   0.088288    -0.122372
    -> v4-bm25-k1big  (b: 0 -> 0.75)          0.151143    +0.062856
    -> v4-bm25        (k1: linear -> 1.2)     0.245760    +0.094617
                                                          ----------
                                                          +0.035100
    registered v4 - v3                                    +0.035100  identical

  Sum and registered difference are +0.03510010508476688 on both sides — the
  same float, not the same rounding.

  AND IT OVERTURNS THE OBVIOUS READING OF THE HEADLINE.

  BM25's SCORING FORM, ON ITS OWN, IS A CATASTROPHE HERE: -0.1224, a 58%
  regression against v3 and well below v1's 0.1402. 903 queries move and v3 is
  ahead on 741 of them. Stripped of both corrections, BM25 is linear tf with no
  length normalisation at all — so long documents dominate every ranking, which
  is the exact failure BM25 was invented to fix.

  THE RUNG IS THE TWO KNOBS, NOT THE FORMULA. Length normalisation is +0.0629
  and tf saturation is +0.0946, and together they turn a -0.1224 regression
  into a +0.0351 win. "BM25 beats tf-idf cosine" is true and is the wrong
  summary; the true one is that BM25's CORRECTIONS beat the cosine's, and the
  underlying weighting scheme is worse.

  THE FIRST STEP IS IRREDUCIBLE AND THAT IS STATED RATHER THAN FAKED. It
  bundles (1), (2) and (3). Splitting them needs a retriever carrying idfPower,
  a v3-flavoured idfVariant and norm: 'l2' — a superset-of-everything scorer
  that is neither v3 nor BM25, whose numbers would answer no question at 3.6,
  on a rung §15.1 praised for REMOVING machinery. What the reader gets instead
  is the arithmetic in section 7 (17.66x vs 5.88x rare-term emphasis) and the
  measured price of (3) alone in ablation (b), +0.0456. Pricing the idf
  exponent separately is on the noticed-list, not done.

  MY PREDICTION WAS WRONG ON k1 AND IT IS WORTH SAYING SO. Before the run I
  expected k1 to barely matter, because at tf = 1 and |D| = avgdl the
  saturation factor is exactly 1 for every k1, and 75.8% of (document, term)
  pairs have tf = 1. The reasoning was right and the conclusion was not: k1 is
  the LARGEST chain step at +0.0946. Two reasons, both measurable — 24.2% of
  pairs have tf > 1 and the max is 47, so the tail is real; and the k1 term
  multiplies the LENGTH factor, so k1 also controls how hard b acts. That
  second coupling is visible in the surface (section 9), where the k1 = 0 row
  is flat in b: at k1 = 0 the length term is multiplied by zero and b is inert.

  44% OF THE MARGIN IS SELECTION OPTIMISM, and the 5th percentile is negative.
  2.7's equivalent figure was +1.4e-5 against a margin of +2.3e-4 — 6% — and
  §13.7 could call it a formality because both of that grid's axes were monotone
  PER QUERY, so the argmax was forced by an argument holding on every subset.
  NONE OF THAT TRANSFERS. k1 and b are continuous, they interact, and the
  optimum is interior on both axes, so the split-half estimate is the only
  evidence there is — and it says most of what tuning appears to buy is the
  maximum of 108 noisy estimates.

  The instability is visible directly, without the arithmetic: across 200 dev
  halves the selection lands on TEN different configurations, and the winner on
  full dev is chosen only 65 times out of 200.

  THE TUNED RUN IS BANKED AND IS EXPLORATORY. v4-bm25-tuned, k1 1.6 b 0.75,
  nDCG@8 0.249946, +0.0042 over the default with CI [+0.0015, +0.0070] and its
  p suppressed by the tool. It is not the rung, by a rule declared before the

  documents v3 was pushing out.


11. WHAT THE RUNG EXPOSED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - THE ROADMAP'S PREMISE ABOUT THE INDEX IS FALSE, and correcting it removed a
    task rather than adding one. 3.3 inherited from 2.7 that "k1 and b both
    change the index, so they cannot reuse a cached one" and budgeted ~30 lines
    of runner mode for it. In a standard BM25 the postings hold raw tf, the
    length ratio is b-independent, and both knobs act at scoring time. It WOULD
    have been true of a v3-style implementation that bakes the finished weight
    into the postings (v3-tfidf.js:303), so this is a design decision made
    deliberately and then ENFORCED: buildIndex() never reads k1 or b, the
    dependent normalisation is memoised lazily at first rank(), a test indexes
    at (1.2, 0.75) and (2.5, 0.1) and asserts every persistent structure is
    identical, and sweep-v4.js re-asserts it at corpus scale before the grid
    runs. The runner mode was not written: index() is ~1.0 s against a ~11 s
    search, under 10%, where §13.10 measured 57%.

  - THE `dirty` FLAG NOW CARRIES INFORMATION, and it changed how this session
    had to be committed. 3.2's noticed-list: sidecars are tracked, so writing
    one dirtied the tree and the next run recorded dirty=true forever. Fixed at
    6e006a7 by excluding each tool's OWN OUTPUT and only that —
    results/comparisons/registry.json is deliberately NOT excluded from
    compare-runs' check, because §11.5's enforcement value is that an
    uncommitted registry edit stays visible. The consequence is an ORDER: a
    comparison report reads the run sidecars, so it can only be clean once they
    are committed. Runs at e0420c1, reports naming it. Every sidecar and every
    report in this session records dirty=false, which no artifact in this repo
    had ever done.

  - assertHits HAS A HOLE THE LADDER HAD NOT REACHED. Section 7. Nothing on
    this corpus triggers it; the test corpus that does is four documents long.

  - THE LABEL SCHEME BROKE WHERE 3.2 PREDICTED, and the fix is a split rather
    than a replacement. Param-encoding cannot express continuous axes. Runs
    cited in prose keep descriptive labels (v4-bm25-b0); the 108 grid points are
    indexed v4-p001...v4-p108 with the params in the CSV and manifest, which is
    where §13.9 already put them.

  - THE LOADER COUNT WAS FOUR, NOT THREE. 3.1's noticed-list named
    emit-per-query-scores.js, compare-runs.js and analyse-rungs.js;
    sweep-v1.js carries its own too. scripts/lib/run-io.js exists so
    sweep-v4.js is not a fifth. It consolidates nothing — §10's validated
    evidence chain and §11.1's deliberate second copy both stay put. Still due
    at 3.6.

  - THE GRID GUARD EARNED ITS PLACE ON FIRST USE. sweep-v4.js fails if the
    default configuration is not on the grid, and it fired: an even
    0, 0.1 ... 1.0 ruler does not contain b = 0.75, so every "what did tuning
    buy" margin would have been measured against a point that was not a row.
    b = 0.75 is on the grid deliberately, breaking the even spacing, exactly as
    §13.2 added the shipped 0.15 to a lattice it is not a member of.

  - THE RUNNER AND THE METRICS: NOTHING. All 21 runs re-scored from the written
    run files through metrics.js and asserted against their committed sidecars
    at exact float equality; passed. No new flag was needed.


12. WHAT THIS RUNG DOES NOT ESTABLISH
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - NOT A TEST-SET RESULT. Dev only, until 3.6.
  - NOT HOLM-ADJUSTED. The p is raw; the family is seven and four now exist.
    Like v3 and unlike v2, the outcome is statable in advance.
  - NOT A CLAIM THAT BM25 BEATS TF-IDF COSINE IN GENERAL. It is a claim about
    2,304 cooking questions whose "queries" are whole documents — which is why
    ablation (b) matters so much and would matter far less on keyword queries.
    Section 8's chain says the weighting scheme is not what wins; the two
    corrections are.
  - NOT A TUNED-vs-TUNED COMPARISON. v3 was never swept. If v3 has a knob worth
    turning, nobody has looked, and the +0.0351 is a comparison of two untuned
    configurations, which is a fair fight and not the same as a best-vs-best.
  - NOT A REASON TO SHIP v4 IN THE APP. It is asymmetric (section 3), and
    Phase 4 has to decide what that costs a bidirectional link graph.
  - ABSOLUTES REMAIN LOWER BOUNDS (§5.1). Validation (§10) and incompleteness
    are two separate qualifiers and neither retires the other.
