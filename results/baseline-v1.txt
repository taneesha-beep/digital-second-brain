3. THE idfCorpus DECISION — leave-one-out
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Opened by the 4 Aug correction that the IDF is live, not inert. Both existing
  dev runs used the default; the param was RECORDED in every digest and every
  run file, and was not DECIDED. It is decided here.

  What the three options measure (backend/retrieval/v1-overlap.js:188-227):

    none            df=0, docCount=1, so idf = ln(2)+1 for every term.
                    The brand-new-account path — a user with no other notes.
    leave-one-out   df over every OTHER corpus document, docCount = N-1.
                    Reproduces loadUserCorpus(userId, {excludeId}), which is
                    what all three production callers pass.
    all             df over the whole corpus including the document itself.
                    Corresponds to no shipped call site.

  DECISION: leave-one-out. Three reasons, in order of weight.

  (a) The parity proof is already banked under it. All 116 ranked links in
      results/parity/v1-shipped.txt == v1-harness.txt (83f9e35e…) were produced
      at idfCorpus: 'leave-one-out', and the equivalence is exact rather than
      approximate: df_loo(w,X) = df(w) - [w in X] with docCount = N-1
      (EVALUATION.md §7.1). Choosing anything else would populate this row with
      a configuration that 2.1's byte-identity claim does not cover — the one
      committed artifact tying this harness to the shipped linker would stop
      describing the thing being measured.

  (b) 'none' is a real shipped path but its degenerate corner. With idf
      constant, ranking collapses to count x (1 + ln len), and PRIMER §3.3
      records what that does: the keyword list drifts toward "the longest words
      present". Banking that as the baseline that five later rungs are measured
      against would flatter all five for a reason unrelated to any of them.

  (c) 'all' matches no production caller and lets the query influence its own
      df table. Not the self-retrieval trap — §7.3 keeps the two exclusions
      distinct — but there is no reason to take it.

  NOTHING WAS RE-RUN. The decision landed on the default, so both committed
  sidecars, results/metric-validation.txt, and the cap-ablation comparison all
  remain descriptions of the configuration this row reports. §4 verifies that
  by regeneration rather than asserting it.

  WHAT THIS ROW IS NOT. "How good is the algorithm I shipped" and "how good is
  this scoring function" are not the same question, and this row answers the
  second under the first's rule. Three gaps, each recorded elsewhere:

    - The deployed IDF scope is a user's <=500 OWN notes, unsorted
      (utils/corpus.js:6). This eval's is 27,325 Stack Exchange documents.
      No configuration on this corpus is literally the shipped one, because
      the shipped scope is a property of a notebook that does not exist here.
      What leave-one-out preserves is the shipped RULE — document frequencies
      come from the other documents in the collection you retrieve over, query
      excluded — under a substituted collection.
    - Shipped keyword lists are snapshots from different corpus epochs
      (§7.2; roadmap 4.6). The harness extracts all of them from one corpus
      state, so this measures a CLEANER system than the deployed one.
    - Shipped storage then mangles the ranked list with last-writer-wins
      bidirectional writes. v1-overlap re-expresses linker.service.js:24-36,
      not 40-64 (§7.5) — quantified at 15 differing lines in §7.6.

  So: the shipped scoring function, under the shipped IDF rule, on an external
  corpus. Not "what the deployed app scores."

  COST AT 3.2, NAMED NOW. Roadmap 3.2's stated IDF ablation is "IDF over all
  27,325 documents versus over a <=500-note user slice". This decision spends
  the first arm on the baseline — and the second arm is NOT RUNNABLE on this
  corpus at all, because it contains no users and no <=500-note slices. The
  axis this param actually spans is leave-one-out vs none: "does the rarity
  signal help", not "which scope of rarity signal". 3.2's framing needs
  correcting a second time, and against this baseline that flip is a clean
  one-variable change (idfCorpus only, everything else held).

5. LATENCY — measured five times, and that changed the precision
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  EVALUATION.md §9.4 records a 38% wall-time spread across five runs with
  byte-identical output, which is the stated reason to distrust timings on this
  machine. That spread is on TOTAL WALL TIME, dominated by load + index(). The
  column here wants p95 of per-query search(). Those are different quantities,
  and before this session there was exactly ONE recorded p95 per configuration
  and no repeat measurement at all — the p95's stability was assumed.

  Five consecutive capped dev runs, all producing run-file sha256 9618f1bf…:

    run   p95        p50        mean       max        wall
    ----------------------------------------------------------
      1   1.009166   0.490875   0.531604    2.308208  2642.4
      2   1.067541   0.521333   0.569609   21.328750  2717.9
      3   1.028666   0.502500   0.537926    1.800291  2624.2
      4   1.040917   0.499333   0.539821    2.069583  2629.4
      5   1.036958   0.501916   0.543349    3.069334  2625.3

    p95     min 1.0092  median 1.0370  max 1.0675   spread  5.8%
    p50     min 0.4909  median 0.5019  max 0.5213   spread  6.2%
    mean    min 0.5316  median 0.5398  max 0.5696   spread  7.1%
    max     min 1.8003  median 2.3082  max 21.3287  spread 1085%

  Two independent measurements of the same configuration fall inside that
  range: 1.042458 ms in the committed sidecar (43fb101, a different session)
  and 1.040458 ms in the first re-run of this session. Seven measurements, all
  in [1.009, 1.073].

  SO THE COLUMN READS 1.0 ms. The leading digit is stable across all seven and
  the second is not, so one significant figure is what the measurement supports
  — the same confidence level §9.4 already committed to. Two decimals would
  assert stability in the hundredths that a laptop with a browser open does not
  have.

  THE 38% DOES NOT TRANSFER, and that is worth stating rather than leaving as
  an inherited caveat. A p95 over 2,304 samples is a far more stable statistic
  than a single wall-clock total: 5.8% against 38%. Run 2 shows why directly —
  one query took 21.3 ms (almost certainly a GC pause), which moved max by an
  order of magnitude and the mean by 7%, and moved p95 by 0.03 ms. max is not a
  quotable statistic here; p95 is, at one figure.

  index() is reported separately and never amortised into the per-query
  number: ~1.29-1.31 s across these five runs, once per run rather than once
  per query.

  The cap cannot be said to affect latency in either direction. Mechanically it
  should not — rank() collects every above-threshold candidate and the wrapper
  sorts before slicing, so cost tracks candidate count, not the cap — and the
  uncapped run's p95 (1.024 ms this session, 1.072 ms at 43fb101) straddles the
  capped range.


6. WHY THE CAPPED RUN POPULATES THE ROW
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  v1-overlap (cap 8) is what is deployed. EVALUATION.md §8.4 committed this in
  writing before any number existed: the uncapped run is a diagnostic and is
  never quoted as the shipped algorithm.

  The column headers do not determine the row on their own. nDCG@8, P@8 and R@8
  are identical across both runs — and §11.6 upgraded that from "four decimal
  places" to EXACT FLOAT EQUALITY on all 2,304 queries individually. What
  differs is MRR@10 (0.1520 capped vs 0.1538 uncapped) and, within noise, the
  latency. Capped is the shipped number in both.

  The uncapped run is not discarded: it is EVALUATION.md §9.3's ablation table,
  §11.6's registered comparison, and its own committed sidecar. It is not a
  ladder row, because a second "v1" would leave every Phase 3 rung comparing
  itself against an ambiguous baseline.
