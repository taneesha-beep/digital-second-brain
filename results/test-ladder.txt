  EVERY RUNG IS LOWER ON TEST. That is a property of the two samples, not of
  the retrievers: the splits are a uniform random partition of one query set
  (§4.2), so a rung has no way to be systematically worse on one of them. The
  shared component is the KEY — test's queries carry a slightly thinner answer
  key, and §5.3 is why per-query scores are not comparable across corpus age.
  Section 3 measures it rather than leaving it as an explanation.

  The direction is consistent with the drop and the SIZE of the effect is not
  established here — this is a description of the two keys, not a decomposition
  of the gap. What it rules out is the reading that would matter: a rung being
  genuinely worse on unseen data, which cannot be what a random partition of
  one query set produces.
