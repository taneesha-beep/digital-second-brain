The first rung whose difference this key can resolve. v3 beats v2 by 0.0680
nDCG@8, 95% CI [0.0566, 0.0797], p < 0.0001 — and it is the first comparison
in this project to clear Holm's alpha/7 = 0.00714, by more than two orders of
magnitude.

What the rung is: PRIMER.md §3.4's observation, acted on. The shipped algorithm
computes a real rarity signal, uses it to pick ten words, and then throws every
number away. v3 stops throwing them away, and stops picking ten.

It is NOT "the IDF finally gets a corpus" — that premise was corrected on
4 Aug 2026 and the label v3-tfidf, not v3-idf, is part of the correction.

3. WHAT v3 IS, AND THE THREE PARAMS IT DID NOT INHERIT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  w(t) = tf(t) . idf(t), every term, cosine. Four params where v2 had seven —
  the rung REMOVES machinery.

  LEAVE-ONE-OUT IDF IS A GLOBAL PER-TERM CONSTANT, which is what makes this a
  real cosine rather than two vectors in differently-scaled spaces. A document
  only holds terms it CONTAINS, and for those df_loo = df - 1 always:

      idf_loo(t, D) = ln((N-1+1) / ((df-1)+1)) + 1 = ln(N / df) + 1

  with no dependence on D. Checked against v1's own arithmetic over 500 corpus
  documents: max |delta| = 0. Measured idf range on cooking: [2.6688, 11.2156]
  — even "recipe" at df 5,150 carries 2.67, so no term is suppressed to
  nothing.

  THE ADMISSION RULE COULD NOT BE INHERITED, and that decision is this rung's
  methodological content, as the threshold decision was v2's.

  v1 admitted on threshold, v2 on minShared, both with cap. A cosine is
  CONTINUOUS, so §13.2's argument — that the threshold grid IS the achievable
  score lattice, which is what made 2.7's sweep exhaustive rather than a sample
  of 342 points — DOES NOT TRANSFER. v1 has 32 achievable scores and v2 has 64.
  v3 has no such set, so no threshold value is defensible and none is inherited.

  DECISION: admit every candidate sharing a term, rank by cosine, return top k.

    threshold       DROPPED, not carried inert as v2 carried it at 0. v2's
                    reason was that a future sweep should be a param flip; that
                    presumes a lattice to sweep. A v3 threshold study needs a
                    NEW argument first, and a file edit is the right friction.

    scorePrecision  DROPPED. In v1, toFixed(4) decided MEMBERSHIP (§7.7); in
                    v2, tie structure over 64 achievable values. Here it would
                    MANUFACTURE ties in a continuous score across ~15,000
                    candidates and hand their order to index.js's lexicographic
                    docid tie-break. Inheriting an artifact, not a rule.

    cap             ABSENT, AND PRE-REGISTERED ABSENT. §14.3 and types.js both
                    recorded "v3 and v4 will carry no cap" at 3.1, before this
                    rung existed. Adding one after seeing 3.1's boundary p is
                    the metric-shopping §11.5 forbids.

    minShared       PRESENT AT 1, WHICH IS INERT — candidates come from the
                    query's postings, so all of them share a term already. It
                    is the ablation handle (section 5c), because the missing
                    rule is a real variable rather than a vacuous one.

  CONSEQUENCE, STATED RATHER THAN SMOOTHED. The registered comparison prices a
  PACKAGE, and compare-runs.js says so in its own output:

    !! 6 variables changed at once: version, cap, minShared, scorePrecision,
       threshold, topN.

  The tool counts PARAM KEYS and three of those six are keys v3 does not
  declare at all. The experiment counts BEHAVIOURS, and there are three:
  similarity function, vocabulary, admission rule. They disagree for a stated
  reason, and the disagreement is printed rather than suppressed — the same
  situation §14.3 recorded at 3.1, one rung larger.

  Section 5 is where the package is taken apart.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Both are below, plus three more. Every one reports "ONE variable changed"

  (a) VOCABULARY TRUNCATION — top-10 vs full. REQUIRED BY THE DONE CRITERION.
      v3-tfidf vs v3-tfidf-top10, one variable: topN.

        nDCG@8   0.210660 vs 0.181728   +0.028932  CI [+0.0229, +0.0350]

      PRIMER.md §3.3 predicted exactly this: "v3 and v4 drop the select-top-10
      stage entirely, so if either wins the bonus is gone regardless."

      Proved rather than argued. v3-tfidf-nolen vs v3-tfidf, one variable:

        nDCG@8 delta EXACTLY 0.000000, and the two run files' retrieval

      The full files differ only in the runid column, because the params — and
      therefore the digest — legitimately differ. §13.3 used the same
      distinction for v1's threshold plateau.

      Measured where it bites, v3-tfidf-top10-nolen vs v3-tfidf-top10:

        nDCG@8   0.184404 vs 0.181728   +0.002676  CI [+0.0001, +0.0053]

      REMOVING THE LENGTH BONUS HELPS, AND THE EVIDENCE IS WEAK. The direction

      the @10 interval straddles it. The defensible sentence is: the bonus is
      not helping, it is worth about a quarter of one percent, and this key
      cannot resolve its sign with confidence. It is dead either way at full
      vocabulary, which is where the rung lives.

      v3-tfidf-minshared2 vs v3-tfidf, one variable: minShared.

        nDCG@8   0.213007 vs 0.210660   +0.002347  CI [-0.0004, +0.0049]

      Recorded so a later phase can take it deliberately, and note it costs one

  CLAUDE.md requires the graph-builder bound stated as O(Sigma_t df_t^2) rather
  than O(N.K), because edge emission from a bucket of size m costs m^2. Dropping
  the truncation multiplies that sum by 21.78x. THE EVAL NEVER PAYS IT: it runs
  2,304 queries, not 27,325^2. What the eval pays is Sigma_{t in q} df_t, and
  that is the 71.4M above — seconds, and the reason section 2's p95 is 8.1 ms
  against v1's 1.0.

  Phase 4 is where 8.12e8 becomes real, and it is recorded here rather than
  discovered there.

  THERE IS NO DOCUMENT-FREQUENCY CUTOFF. If one becomes necessary it will be a
  retriever PARAM that lands in describe(handle).digest, never a silent
  constant — a silent cutoff is the "run that lies about itself" of §13.10. It
  would also be a fifth variable in an already-three-variable comparison.

  rank() COLLECTS EVERY CANDIDATE rather than pre-truncating with ctx.k —
  ~15,000 collect() calls per query where v1 made ~63. The optimisation was
  declined: collecting everything is the version whose equivalence to the
  interface contract needs no argument, because it IS the contract. If it ever
  has to change, the replacement ships with a byte-identity proof against this
  one.


7. QUERY 52326 — a thread from §7.7 and §14.6, closed
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  "What are marshmallows?" / "What are marshmallows and how are they made?"
  After stopwords, ONE distinct token: marshmallows.

    v1  returns 8 documents, every one at strength 1/1 = 1.0000 — the maximum
        the algorithm can emit, ranked above every genuine multi-word match
    v2  returns 0 — minShared 2 is unreachable for a one-term document
    v3  returns 10, top score 0.799187

  §14.6 called this "d = 1" and read it as an artifact of the top-10 selection.
  IT IS NOT: the document has one usable token at FULL vocabulary too, so the
  corner is the document's, not the truncation's. v3 handles it because the
  cosine normalises by the norm — the score becomes "how much of the target is
  about marshmallows", which is bounded below 1 unless the target is about
  nothing else.

  It is also why v3-tfidf-minshared2 (5c) has exactly one zero-result query,
  and it is this one.


8. WHAT THE RUNG EXPOSED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  THE RETRIEVER SOURCE WAS NOT RECORDED ANYWHERE — fixed first, before v3 ran.
  3.1's noticed-list item. The param digest covers {version, params} and
  nothing else, so an edit to v1-overlap.js moves v2's and v3's numbers with no
  digest change. eval/source-digest.js hashes the require closure into every
  sidecar; compare-runs.js re-hashes at report time and names the differing
  FILES. It earned its place on first use: v2's run predated v3's registration
  line and the report said so, naming backend/retrieval/index.js.

  analyse-rungs.js WAS NOT GENERALISED, despite 3.1 saying it was. It read
  handle._state.keywordsById — v1's private representation — and crashed with a
  TypeError on the first rung that does not store keyword lists. Fixed via an
  optional termCount() on the Retriever contract. Two things it was hiding:
    - the quantity is not comparable across rungs (a top-10 length is capped at
      10; a full-vocabulary count runs to 1,021), yet the 6/7 BUCKETING agrees
      on every document — forced, because below 10 the truncation does not bind
    - its histogram printed rows 1..10 only, which under a full-vocabulary
      yardstick is a corner of the distribution that looks complete
  §14.6's published v2-vs-v1 output is unchanged, verified line by line.

  SYMMETRY WAS NOT FREE, and it was a test failure before it was a decision.
  A cosine is symmetric mathematically, but the two directions accumulate the
  same products in different orders and floating-point addition is not
  associative: score(A->B) came out 0.14845859879756534 against ...32. Rather
  than weaken v2's exact-equality claim to a tolerance, term ids are assigned
  in SORTED VOCABULARY order and every vector is stored ascending, so both
  directions visit shared terms identically. Sorted rather than first-seen, or
  corpus order would permute the ids and move the last bits back. Both
  properties are asserted by tests.

  THE RUNNER AND THE METRICS: nothing. Both sides re-scored from the written
  run files through metrics.js and asserted against their committed sidecars at
  exact float equality; passed. No new flag was needed.

  slice(0, null) RETURNS [] — a topN of null reaching a truncation path would
  empty every vector and make every score 0/0, a run of zeroes that looks like
  a finding. Rejected at index() with a test.

  THE `dirty` FLAG IS STRUCTURALLY ALWAYS TRUE. gitProvenance() excludes
  untracked files so that "every run after the first" is not marked dirty by
  its own output — but SIDECARS ARE TRACKED, so writing one dirties the tree
  and the next run records dirty=true anyway. The committed sidecars at 3.1
  already say dirty=true for this reason. Not fixed; noticed.
