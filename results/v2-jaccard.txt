3. THE THRESHOLD IS NOT INHERITED — the methodological content of the rung
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Jaccard silently redefines what a threshold means. For two ten-keyword notes
  sharing s words the union is 20 - s, so (all values through toFixed(4),
  which is what decides membership at the boundary, §7.7):

      s        v1 = s/10        v2 = s/(20-s)
      1          0.1000           0.0526
      2          0.2000  admitted 0.1111  REJECTED
      3          0.3000           0.1765  admitted

  The SAME 0.15 demands THREE shared words under Jaccard where it demanded
  TWO under v1. Keeping the numeral is therefore not a one-variable change: it
  moves the similarity function AND tightens the rule, and §13.5 already
  priced that tightening on v1's own axis at 12.2% of nDCG@8 and 42x the
  zero-result queries. §9 measures what it costs in Jaccard's own coordinates.

  DECISION: hold the BEHAVIOUR constant, not the numeral. The numeral was
  never a setting — §13.3 measured that 0.15 = 3/20 is not an achievable v1
  score at all, so "> 0.15" keeps byte-for-byte what "> 0.1429" keeps. It is a
  LABEL for the rule "share at least 2 of 10", and the rule is what v2 carries
  across, as the length-independent integer minShared = 2.

  WHY minShared AND NOT A SCALAR THRESHOLD IN THE EQUIVALENT RANGE. The scalar
  thresholds reproducing "s >= 2" at the modal pair are [1/19, 2/18) =
  [0.0526, 0.1111). But a Jaccard threshold leaks the TARGET's length into the
  admission rule, which v1's never did. Enumerated over all 385 (a, b, s) with
  a, b <= 10 (asserted in tests/retrieval.v2-symmetry.test.js):

      v1(0.15) vs minShared 2, a >= 7:  210 agree,  0 differ
      v1(0.15) vs scalar 0.1,  a >= 7:               6 differ
        a=7,b<=3,s=1 · a=8,b<=2,s=1 · a=9,b=1,s=1

  WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED. Exact equivalence is impossible: v1's rule does not read
  |B| at all and Jaccard's necessarily does. The residual is the d <= 6
  stratum — 25 of 2,304 dev queries, 1.09% (§13.8) — where v1 links on a single
  shared word and v2 does not. v2 is strictly STRICTER there, so its candidate
  set is a subset of v1's on those 25. One-directional, bounded, and reported
  stratified in §6 rather than left as a confound.

  THE SCORE LATTICE MOVES WITH IT. v1's achievable scores are s/d for
  1 <= s <= d <= 10 — the Farey fractions of order 10, 32 values, which is what
  made 2.7's threshold sweep exhaustive. v2's are s/(a+b-s) for
  1 <= s <= min(a,b) <= 10, which is { s/u : 1 <= s <= u <= 20 - s }: 64
  distinct values, denominators to 19, floor at 1/19 = 0.0526 instead of 1/10.
  Derived from the algebra and then checked against what the retriever emits,
  as §13.2 did for v1. An exhaustive v2 threshold sweep is therefore a
  DIFFERENT and larger grid, and 3.1 does not run one: a swept v2 measured
  against an unswept v1 is not the registered experiment.


4. SYMMETRY, PROVED RATHER THAN ASSERTED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The point of the rung. PRIMER.md §3.5 records two compounding quirks: the
  score is asymmetric, and linker.service.js:41 writes the source's strength
  into the target's record, so the STORED graph depends on save order. Phase
  4.2 fixes the storage half; this rung removes the algorithm half, so there is
  no longer a "which direction" for storage to get wrong.

  tests/retrieval.v2-symmetry.test.js, on the 34-document fixture:

    - score(A->B) === score(B->A) at EXACT float equality (toBe, not
      toBeCloseTo) on every pair, both halves checked: B must be REACHABLE
      from A exactly when A is reachable from B, and then the scores agree.
    - v1 is put through the identical check and FAILS it, so the asymmetry
      being removed is measured rather than assumed.
    - and a three-document case with deliberately unequal keyword lists, so
      the property is the formula's and not the fixture's.

  The shared TERMS are equal as a set, not as a sequence: sharedKeywords is
  built in target order, which is v1's convention (§7.6) and is kept so a
  v1-vs-v2 diff of explain fields stays comparable. The test asserts set
  equality and not sequence equality, because sequence equality is a claim
  this does not make.


5. THE REGISTERED COMPARISON, AND WHY THE p IS NOT THE ANSWER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  results/comparisons/v2-jaccard-vs-v1-overlap.dev.txt, registered at 2.5 as
  ladder-v1-v2 (a = v2-jaccard, b = v1-overlap, split dev, primary nDCG@8).

    metric        mean A     mean B        A - B    95% CI              differ
    ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
     nDCG@1      0.1008    0.0971     +0.0038   [-0.0003, +0.0080]     25
     nDCG@5      0.1307    0.1291     +0.0017   [-0.0011, +0.0045]     94
    *nDCG@8      0.1427    0.1402     +0.0025   [+0.0000, +0.0050]    141   p=0.0482
     nDCG@10     0.1425    0.1400     +0.0025   [+0.0000, +0.0050]    141
     P@8         0.0381    0.0375     +0.0005   [-0.0002, +0.0012]     45
     R@8         0.1921    0.1887     +0.0035   [-0.0003, +0.0074]     45
     MRR@10      0.1546    0.1520     +0.0027   [-0.0003, +0.0057]    124

  THE SENTENCE: v2-jaccard beats v1-overlap by 0.0025 nDCG@8,
                95% CI [0.0000, 0.0050], p = 0.0482.

  THREE REASONS NOT TO READ THAT AS "v2 WINS", in ascending order of weight.

  (a) The CI lower bound is +0.000028. The interval clears zero by three parts
      in a hundred thousand. Every other metric's interval straddles zero.

  (b) B = 10,000 CANNOT RESOLVE THIS p, and §11.3 said so in advance — it is
      the case that section built --resamples for. Six seeds at the registered
      B, and three of them land on the wrong side of 0.05:

        20260804  0.0482      20260807  0.0478
        20260805  0.0514      20260808  0.0504
        20260806  0.0504      20260809  0.0504

      Driven to B = 1,000,000, Monte Carlo error +/- 0.0002:

        20260804  0.0492      20260805  0.0494      20260807  0.0491

      So the true ASL is ~0.0492 and it clears 0.05 by 0.0008. The REGISTERED
      value stays 0.0482 (seed 20260804, B = 10,000) — the higher-B runs are a
      Monte Carlo PRECISION measurement, explicitly authorised by §11.3, not a
      re-test, and not a search for a smaller number.

  (c) HOLM IS THE ONE THAT SETTLES IT. The family is seven (§11.5) and Holm
      requires the smallest raw p to clear alpha/7 = 0.00714. A raw p of 0.049
      is nowhere near that. When the family completes at the end of Phase 3
      this comparison will NOT be declared significant, and that is knowable
      now rather than at 3.6.

  THE SHAPE OF THE DIFFERENCE IS THE FRAGILE ONE. 141 of 2,304 queries move —
  and v1 is ahead on 91 of them against v2's 50. v2 wins less often and wins
  bigger: mean gain +0.298951 on its 50, mean loss -0.100932 on v1's 91. That
  is the opposite shape from the cap ablation, which was 61 of 61 in one
  direction (§11.6) and whose significance came from perfect consistency. Here
  the effect is a minority of large wins, which is exactly the kind that does
  not replicate.

  THE HONEST SENTENCE: on this key, at this depth, symmetric scoring made no
  difference that survives the pre-registered protocol. That is a real result.


6. THE MECHANISM — measured, not inferred
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Every number in this section regenerates with

    cd backend && npm run analyse:rungs -- --a v2-jaccard --b v1-overlap --split dev

  which is read-only, re-derives from the written run files through the same
  metrics.js validated at 2.4, and asserts its own aggregate against both
  committed sidecars at exact float equality before printing anything.

  v1's score is s/a with a fixed per query, so v1 ranks by s ALONE, ties broken
  lexicographically on the id. v2's is s/(a+b-s), which is increasing in s and
  DECREASING in the target's keyword count b. So Jaccard promotes short
  targets — and genuinely reorders across s levels, not just within them: at
  a = 10, a target with s=2,b=2 scores 0.2000 and outranks one with s=3,b=10 at
  0.1765.

  §13.8 measured that 26,258 of 27,325 corpus documents (96.1%) carry exactly
  ten keywords, so for most candidate pairs b = 10, the union is constant, and
  v2 is a monotone transform of v1 — identical ranking. Only the other 3.9% can
  move. That is the whole effect, and here it is:

    keyword length of RETRIEVED documents, top 8, whole dev run
      d      v1        v2      delta
      3       3        24       +21
      4       1        15       +14
      5      14       134      +120
      6      48       226      +178
      7      97       606      +509
      8     219       982      +763
      9     289      1203      +914
     10   17248     14702     -2546
      -----------------------------
     d<=9   671      3190     4.75x

  v2 retrieves 4.75 times as many short documents. The rung does exactly what
  the formula says it does, and the metric barely notices.

  STRATIFIED BY QUERY KEYWORD COUNT, the split §3 predicts:

    stratum   n      v1 nDCG@8     v2 nDCG@8     moved   contribution to mean
    d <= 6    25     0.19307917    0.23585211      2         +0.00046412
    d >= 7  2279     0.13958650    0.14164592    139         +0.00203707
                                                            -------------
                                                             +0.00250119

  The two v1 figures reproduce §13.8's stratum means exactly, which is the
  cross-check that the stratification is the same one.

  The d <= 6 stratum is where the admission rules differ, and it moved on only
  2 of its 25 queries — so its +0.043 is two queries, and NOTHING should be
  concluded from it. Note also that it moved UP despite v2 being strictly
  stricter there: the gain is reordering, not admission. 81% of the overall
  difference comes from d >= 7, where the admitted sets are identical to v1 by
  construction and only the scoring function differs. That is the number the
  rung actually earned.

  THE EXTRA ZERO-RESULT QUERY, all the way down. v1 has 11 (§8.3), v2 has 12,
  and the difference is one query: 52326, which extracts exactly ONE keyword.
  v1 scores any document sharing that word at s/1 = 1.0000 — the maximum
  possible strength — and returns 8 of them. v2 needs two shared words and a
  one-keyword note can never have two. §7.7's "d <= 6 links on a single shared
  word" understates this corner: at d = 1 the shipped algorithm links on one
  word AT FULL CONFIDENCE.

8. THE cap DEFECT, FIXED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Opened by 2.7 (§13.10) and in scope here because 3.1 touches the interface.
  index.js read the cap as `Number.isFinite(handle.params.cap) ? ... : Infinity`
  and Number.isFinite does not coerce, so a cap arriving as the STRING "8" fell
  through to Infinity: the run was silently UNCAPPED while describe(handle)
  faithfully recorded cap:"8". A run that lies about itself — the exact failure
  the --param JSON parsing exists to prevent, surviving one layer further down.

  cap is now validated at index() — null or a positive integer, anything else
  throws — so the failure is at handle construction rather than after 2,304
  queries of a wrong run. A non-integer is rejected for the same reason in a
  quieter form: slice truncates, so cap 8.5 behaves as 8 while the digest says
  8.5.

  Nothing moves. Both spellings every committed run uses — 8 and null — resolve
  exactly as before, results/parity/ still hashes to 83f9e35e…, and a retriever
  declaring no cap at all (v3 and v4 will not) is still uncapped by absence.
  Five tests cover it, including the regression stated as behaviour: cap 1
  returns one result and cap "1" now throws instead of returning three.


9. THE NAIVE PORT — what keeping 0.15 would have cost
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Run because §3's argument deserved a measurement rather than an assertion.
  v2-jaccard-naive is the literal one-line port: minShared 1, threshold 0.15,
  everything else identical.

    label              nDCG@8     nDCG@10   R@8      MRR@10   zero-result  lines
    v1-overlap         0.140167   0.140025  0.1887   0.1520     11 (0.5%)  17,919
    v2-jaccard         0.142668   0.142514  0.1921   0.1546     12 (0.5%)  17,892
    v2-jaccard-naive   0.124981   0.124864  0.1581   0.1420    452 (19.6%)  9,104

  Keeping the numeral would have cost 10.8% of nDCG@8 against v1 and
  multiplied zero-result queries by 41 — and every bit of that is the
  admission rule, not Jaccard. §13.5 predicted it from v1's own axis: raising
  v1 to "3 shared words" costs 12.2% and gives 461 zero-result queries. Measured
  here in Jaccard's coordinates: 10.8% and 452. The small gap between the two is
  itself the target-length dependence Jaccard adds, which v1's axis cannot show.

  So a v2 that had "just changed the denominator" would have reported a 10.8%
  regression and attributed it to symmetric scoring. That is the failure this
  rung's threshold decision avoids, and it is now a number rather than an
  argument.

  EXPLORATORY. The pair is not registered, compare-runs.js suppresses its
  p-value, and registry.json was not touched this session.


10. WHAT THIS FILE DOES NOT ESTABLISH
==============================================================================
  - NOT a test-set result. dev only, until 3.6.
  - NOT significant under the pre-registered protocol. Raw p 0.049 against a
    Holm bound of 0.00714 at family size 7. §5.
  - NOT a one-variable change in the param diff, and compare-runs.js says so
    in its own output: version, threshold and minShared all move. Three
    coordinates express one behavioural change plus the bounded 1.09%
    residual of §3. The tool counts params; the experiment counts behaviours;
    they disagree here for a stated reason and the disagreement is printed
    rather than suppressed.
  - NOT a claim that Jaccard is a better similarity function in general. It is
    a claim about 2,304 cooking questions whose keyword lists are ten words
    long 96% of the time — the regime where Jaccard and the overlap
    coefficient are nearly the same function. A corpus with variable document
    lengths would exercise this far harder, and a note-taking app's notes are
    exactly that (§7.7).
  - NOT a latency comparison. §7.
  - Absolutes remain LOWER BOUNDS (§5.1). Validation (§10) and incompleteness
    (§5.1) are two separate qualifiers and neither retires the other.
