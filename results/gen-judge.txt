PHASE 5.6 — LLM-AS-JUDGE FOR GROUNDEDNESS, WITH A HUMAN AGREEMENT CHECK

  A rubric-based, claim-level groundedness judgement, graded by a model that
  is NOT the model being judged, and checked against hand labels.

  Produced by:  cd backend && npm run eval:judge -- --write
  PURE — no key, no network, nothing under data/. Reads only results/.

  PRIMER §5.3a: a rate needs its denominator and a score needs its null, and
  neither is ever printed alone. §32.5 reached the same conclusion for the
  LEXICAL support metric, where a bare 0.283 read as failure until the 0.119
  floor gave it a scale. A groundedness rate has exactly that problem: it is
  a judgement about how strict a rubric is as much as about the system, and
  only the gap separates the two.

  KAPPA IS REPORTED WITH P_o BESIDE IT AND THAT IS NOT DECORATION. Cohen's
  kappa collapses toward 0 when both raters put nearly everything in one
  category, however often they agree — the kappa paradox. A bare kappa in
  that regime understates agreement and a reader cannot tell.

  Here P_o - kappa is 0.394, which IS that regime.
    human marginals   0:29  1:21  2:0
    judge marginals   0:40  1:7  2:3

  WHAT KAPPA CANNOT SAY: it measures whether two raters agree, not whether
  either is right. A judge that reproduces a human's systematic mistake
  scores well here. The human is one rater — me — so there is no
  inter-HUMAN agreement figure and no way to separate rubric ambiguity from
  rater error. That needs a second human and is not bought here.

  This is what stops "the judge is lenient" being the automatic reading of a

  in completion_tokens_details, so the field gen-v5 relies on is blind to it.
  The run disables reasoning; the count above is what says it stayed disabled.
  §28.9's class: the model is the one input with no checksum.

==============================================================================
H. WHAT THESE NUMBERS CANNOT SAY
==============================================================================

  KAPPA MEASURES AGREEMENT, NOT CORRECTNESS. One human, no inter-human
  agreement figure, so rubric ambiguity and rater error are not separable.

  THE ITEM SET IS CENSORED AT THE CALL LEVEL, AND THIS IS THE LIMITATION MOST
  LIKELY TO BE QUOTED PAST. gen-v5's 30 completed calls include 7 that
  truncated at the INHERITED max_tokens: 2048 ceiling. A truncated pack parses
  to nothing and contributes ZERO items, and those packs are systematically
  the LONGER seeds: 140.4 mean words against 87.3. So groundedness is
  measured over 23 of 30 seeds, skewed short. The items themselves are
  intact — every contributing call finished with `stop` — but the seed mix is
  not the golden set's. ROADMAP 5.9 owns the ceiling.

  NO NOISE FLOOR UNDER THE ITEMS. Inherited from gen-v5 and not fixed here:
  one draw per seed at temperature 0.4, no repeats. §28.8 measured 32.1% of
  examQs cells flipping verdict on a re-draw and §32.10 states that no gen-v5
  figure may be compared against a later one until repeats are bought. The
  JUDGE runs at temperature 0, so it adds little of its own — but temperature 0
  is not determinism, and nothing here claims a judged run would replay exactly.

  CORRELATED ERROR IS NOT RULED OUT BY A DIFFERENT VENDOR. Two models trained
  on overlapping text can be confidently wrong about the same claim. A
  different family lowers self-preference; it does not eliminate shared priors.

  NOT A RETRIEVER COMPARISON. That is 5.7, and the stamped retriever/digest on
  every gen-v5 ledger row is what it will need.

  THE NEIGHBOURS ARE RETRIEVED OVER 27,325 CORPUS DOCUMENTS, not a <=500-note
  user slice, and the seeds are Stack Exchange questions shaped as Notes.
  §12.2's gap, tenth instance.
